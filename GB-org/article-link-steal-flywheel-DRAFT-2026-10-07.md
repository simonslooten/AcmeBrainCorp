## Paste-ready LinkedIn body

```
Most people bookmark the AI-agent thread and never open it again.

I send it to my Chief of Staff bot. The org decides what to steal, what to skip, and what to park. Then one small rule gets locked, or nothing does.

That is the whole flywheel: link in, steal or skip, rule locks, org compounds. It gets a bit stronger every time, without getting bigger.

To be clear: we did not invent multi-agent orgs. Plenty of people run a Chief of Staff bot. This post is about what happens to the links.

---

How a link gets processed

I drop a link into chat, usually an X post or an article. The CoS routes it to whoever owns that ground. That specialist reads the full text, not a summary, and writes one page: what the post claims, what we already do, the real gap if there is one, a short list of what is worth taking, and a longer list of what we skip, each with a reason.

Then I decide. Nothing locks without my yes.

The steal is almost always thin. A sentence. A check. One line in a checklist. That is on purpose. Thin steals go into places that already exist: the hire bar (what a bot must prove before it gets more scope), the material bar (what "done" means before anything leaves draft), or the routing file (who owns what, and who may ping me). They do not become new bots. They do not become new tools.

Every verdict lands as one line in a single index, newest first. If a link did not change a rule, it did not change the org.

---

Five that stuck

A post about getting winning runs out of chat. We took three small things. Capture the method while the good run is still on screen. Test it on a second, different input before calling it a skill; otherwise you saved a transcript. And a checklist before any routine goes live: named owner, time zone, input source, failure policy, approval boundary. All three now sit in the hire bar.

A post dressed up as an academic paper about agents as an operating system. The paper did not check out: we found no trace of it, so we do not cite it. The idea underneath was sound, so we took that. Three confidence lanes. High confidence and low consequence: do it alone. Medium: buy new evidence (a second source, a hard check) instead of re-running the same prompt. Low confidence or high consequence: me. Plus one line we now use when briefing a bot: the model searches; rules decide what may leave.

A dependency-audit prompt. Our weekly huddle already asked what the CoS got wrong. Nothing asked where work was waiting on me. Now, once a month, in a slot that already existed, the CoS lists where things stalled on me and labels each line: keep me in, teach, systemise, automate, or escalate only. With a hard floor: sending, posting, paying, signing, deleting and calendar invites stay "keep me in", whatever the audit says. Nothing changes until I go through it line by line.

A thread selling a research setup. We skipped every tool in it and took the discipline. A summary is a lead, never proof. Three independent sources or one primary, otherwise it is marked unconfirmed. Findings get a review date. That rule is also what made the fake-paper call quick.

A list of twenty tips. One of them, a bot that runs cost hygiene on a schedule, became a real seat: Lirili, our cost bot, with a weekly Friday audit that stays quiet when nothing material moved. The other nineteen did not make it. That ratio is about right.

---

What gets skipped (most of it)

This is the part that keeps a flywheel from turning into a swarm.

Most links produce one or two lines and a long skip list. "Add a bot for this" is the most common suggestion in these posts and the one we take least. A post about a hundred-plus connectors: nothing to steal. Paid research engines, scrapers, a trio of skeptic agents: skipped. Someone else's charter pasted in wholesale: skipped.

Cool but not cash gets parked, not deleted. An AI trading desk looked impressive. Speculation is not an invoice. Parked, with a note on what would reopen it. Same for a decision-layer product we liked: parked until there is a bounded or paying reason to reopen it.

The test for a new seat is boring. Is there a cash lane? Can it pass the hire bar? Can its first work be checked in about thirty seconds? A tip on its own never clears that.

---

Why it compounds

Each steal makes the next review cheaper. The research rule made the fake paper easy to spot. The confidence lanes and the hard floor gave the dependency audit its guard rails. The hire bar is where every new idea gets tested against everything already locked.

So the org gets stronger mostly by getting stricter in small, written ways. Same roster, sharper rules.

The shape, briefly. A Chief of Staff routes. Specialists sit under leads. Bonica, our QA bot, checks material work before anyone calls it done. One bot handles anything outward, and only after QA clears it and I say yes. Drafts get parked, not sent.

---

Interrupt rule

I do not get pinged unless it is money or the product, and only the CoS pings me. A steal/skip page does not interrupt anything. It waits until I decide.

Outward stays with me. Nothing in this loop publishes, sends or pays on its own. This post included: drafted by the org, checked by QA, posted by me.

---

Bookmarks are a reading list. A steal/skip is a decision.

If you send your agents links, ask for one page back: what we already do, what is actually new, what we skip and why. Then lock one line, or none.

I am curious which single line others actually locked from a post. Not the stack. The line.
```
