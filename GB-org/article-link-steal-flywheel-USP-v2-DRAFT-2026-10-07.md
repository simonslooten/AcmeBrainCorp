## Paste-ready LinkedIn body

```
Most people playing with AI agents are still bookmarking the thread.

We are past that. Further than 99% of what I see in the wild. That is my read as an operator, not a survey. And it is not because we run more bots. It is because what we read turns into rules, and nothing leaves the building until it has survived its own critique.

To be clear: we did not invent multi-agent orgs. Plenty of people run a Chief of Staff bot. The gap is in what happens after the link, and before the send.

---

What "further" means

When I see a good post about AI agents, I do not save it. I send it to my Chief of Staff bot. The specialist who owns that ground reads the full text and writes one page back: what the post claims, what we already do, the real gap, and a short list of what's worth taking. Then a longer list of what we skip, each with a reason.

Then I decide. One thin rule gets locked, or nothing does.

The rule lands in a place that already exists: the hire bar (what a bot must prove before it gets more scope), the material bar (what "done" means before anything leaves draft), or the routing file (who owns what, who may ping me). It does not turn into a new bot or a new tool.

So the org compounds without growing. Same roster, sharper rules.

Most setups I see do the opposite. Every good thread adds a tool, a prompt or a bot. Give it a few months and they have a swarm nobody trusts and a bookmark folder nobody opens.

---

Three receipts

A post that dressed itself up as an academic paper about agents as an operating system. A rule we had locked earlier says a summary is a lead, never proof: three independent sources or one primary, otherwise it is marked unconfirmed. We found no trace of the paper, so we do not cite it. The idea underneath was sound, so we kept that: high confidence and low stakes, the bot acts alone. Medium, it goes and gets new evidence. Low confidence or high stakes, it comes to me.

A prompt for auditing where work waits on a human. Now, once a month, in a slot that already existed, the Chief of Staff lists where things stalled on me and labels each line: keep me in, teach, systemise, automate, or escalate only. With a hard floor: sending, posting, paying, signing, deleting and calendar invites stay "keep me in", whatever the audit says.

A list of twenty tips. One became a real seat: a cost bot that runs a weekly check and stays quiet when nothing material moved. The other nineteen did not make it.

That ratio is the real skill. Most of the work is saying no in writing.

---

What we refuse

"Add a bot for this" is the most common suggestion in these posts, and the one we take least. Connector counts, paid research engines, someone else's charter pasted in wholesale: skipped. Cool but not cash gets parked with a note on what would reopen it. An AI trading desk looked impressive. Speculation is not an invoice.

A new seat has to clear a boring test. Is there a cash lane? Can it pass the hire bar? Can its first work be checked in about thirty seconds? A tip on its own never clears that.

---

The bar nobody sees

This is the other half of the gap, and it is less glamorous than any bot.

Most people ship the first draft the model gave them. Maybe they reread it once. In our org, a draft does not leave its author until it has argued with itself.

Every piece of material work (a brief, a letter, a proposal, this post) runs an eight-step self-critique first. The author names its three worst weaknesses, plays its own harshest critic, answers three domain experts, lists every assumption it made, asks what a 10 out of 10 would need, hunts down anything vague or contradictory, rereads it as the actual reader, and then takes one more minute for one last change. And then it rewrites. Not notes in the margin: the draft itself changes, and the top fixes get written down.

That ladder is why the 99% line above says "my read, not a survey". An earlier version said it bare.

Then a separate quality bot, one that did not write it, checks it against the bar and says clear or not yet. Only then does it reach me.

A few dull habits sit around that. Every piece carries its own tape: sources, guesses, what was skipped, and the evidence. A guess is labelled as a guess, never quietly filled in. Every job starts with a short contract: the result, the inputs, what "done" means in checkable terms, and who approves. And confidence is not done. "Pretty sure it's finished" counts for nothing; passed gates count.

None of this is clever. It is just enforced, every time.

---

Why you can trust it

This is the part I'd look for if I were buying.

I don't get pinged unless it's money or the product, and only the Chief of Staff pings me. A steal/skip page waits until I decide.

Nothing in this loop sends, posts or pays on its own. That includes this post: drafted by the org, critiqued by its author, checked by QA, posted by me.

Keeping me in on purpose is not a limitation. It is what makes the rest safe to speed up.

---

Most teams I talk to already use ChatGPT or an agent or two. What they lack isn't another tool. They lack the loop that turns what they read into a decision, a decision into a rule, and a first draft into something they would sign.

If your bookmarks never become a rule: DM me "steal". I'll send the one-page shape we use (link → steal/skip → lock one line or none). No deck.
```
