Most people playing with AI agents are still bookmarking the thread.
We are past that. Further than almost anything I see in the wild. That is my read as an operator, not a survey. It is not because we run more bots. It is because what we read turns into a rule, and nothing leaves the building until it has survived its own critique.
We did not invent the multi-agent org. Plenty of people run a Chief of Staff bot. The gap is what happens after the link, and before the send.
When I see a good post about agents, I do not save it. I send it to the Chief of Staff. The specialist who owns that ground reads the full text and writes one page back: what the post claims, what we already do, the real gap, a short list of what is worth taking, and a longer list of what we skip, each with a reason. Then I decide. One thin rule gets locked, or nothing does.
The rule lands somewhere that already exists. The hire bar, which is what a bot must prove before it gets more scope. The material bar, which is what "done" means before anything leaves draft. Or the routing file: who owns what, and who may ping me. It does not become a new bot or a new tool. The org compounds without growing. Same roster, sharper rules.
Most setups I see do the opposite. Every good thread adds a tool, a prompt or a bot. Give it a few months and they have a swarm nobody trusts, and a bookmark folder nobody opens.
Three receipts.
A post dressed itself up as an academic paper about agents as an operating system. A rule we had already locked says a summary is a lead, never proof: three independent sources or one primary, otherwise it is marked unconfirmed. We found no trace of the paper, so we do not cite it. The idea underneath was sound, so we kept that. High confidence and low stakes, the bot acts alone. Medium, it goes and gets new evidence. Low confidence or high stakes, it comes to me.
A prompt for auditing where work waits on a human. Once a month, in a slot that already existed, the Chief of Staff lists where things stalled on me and labels each line: keep me in, teach, systemise, automate, or escalate only. With a hard floor. Sending, posting, paying, signing, deleting and calendar invites stay "keep me in", whatever the audit says.
A list of twenty tips. One became a real seat: a cost bot that runs a weekly check and stays quiet when nothing material moved. The other nineteen did not make it. That ratio is the skill. Most of the work is saying no in writing.
"Add a bot for this" is the most common suggestion in these posts, and the one we take least. Connector counts, paid research engines, someone else's charter pasted in wholesale: skipped. Cool but not cash gets parked, with a note on what would reopen it. An AI trading desk looked impressive. Speculation is not an invoice.
A new seat has to clear a boring test. Is there a cash lane? Can it pass the hire bar? Can its first work be checked in about thirty seconds? A tip on its own never clears that.
The other half of the gap is less glamorous than any bot. Most people ship the first draft the model gave them. Maybe they reread it once. Here a draft does not leave its author until it has argued with itself.
Every piece of material work, a brief, a letter, a proposal, this post, runs the same pass first. The author names its three worst weaknesses, plays its own harshest critic, answers three domain experts, lists every assumption, asks what a 10 out of 10 would need, hunts down anything vague or contradictory, rereads it as the actual reader, and then takes one more minute for one last change. Then it rewrites. Not notes in the margin. The draft itself changes, and the top fixes get written down. That is why the line above says "my read, not a survey". An earlier version said it bare.
Then a separate quality check, by someone who did not write it, says clear or not yet. Only then does it reach me.
A few dull habits sit around that. Every piece carries its own tape: sources, guesses, what was skipped, and the evidence. A guess is labelled as a guess, never quietly filled in. Every job starts with a short contract: the result, the inputs, what "done" means in checkable terms, and who approves. Confidence is not done. "Pretty sure it's finished" counts for nothing. Passed gates count.
None of this is clever. It is just enforced, every time.
I don't get pinged unless it's money or the product, and only the Chief of Staff pings me. A steal-or-skip page waits until I decide. Nothing in this loop sends, posts or pays on its own. That includes this post: drafted in the org, critiqued by its author, checked, posted by me. Keeping me in on purpose is not a limitation. It is what makes the rest safe to speed up.
Most teams I talk to already use a model or an agent or two. What they lack is not another tool. They lack the loop that turns what they read into a decision, a decision into a rule, and a first draft into something they would sign.
If your bookmarks never become a rule, send me "steal". I'll send the one-page shape we use. Link, steal or skip, lock one line or none. No deck.
