## Paste-ready LinkedIn body

```
Most "how I run my AI agents" posts are still wishlists.

This one is a working system — not a claim that we invented the category.

---

Solo load is real. Short attention is real. Chaos is real. AI is a force multiplier — until it becomes more social overhead: more bots, more pings, more "quick checks," more theatre.

I wanted capacity without another meeting culture inside my laptop. Force multiplier, not a second inbox full of personalities.

So we run two sites under one owner.

**GB — Grok Bot HQ.** A Chief of Staff routes work. Specialists report through leads (business development, revenue ops, search, copy, visual, QA). Only the CoS pings me on completions. Everyone else finishes and files. Cash priority and external gates sit with CoS — not with whoever finished last and felt chatty.

**GX — Grok as the deep-work / COO seat.** Long drafts. Heavy research. Fractional search packs. Not a second CoS. Not a parallel org fighting for my calendar. Load-split is written down: heavy thinking goes to GX; short ops, routing, and send-gates stay on GB. Same company. Different cost and context profiles. That is the point.

Between them sits a boring, useful thing: a shared Drive folder.

PROTOCOL. STATE. LOG. INBOX-GROK. INBOX-COS. Plus inbox / current / archive when a dump is bigger than a note. CoS leads the bridge. Manual trigger in chat is `csx` — a comm check. No side-channel "we agreed in some other chat" counting as shared truth until it lands here. STATE is writable only by CoS or me.

That architecture is platform-independent. Shared files, clear roles, who may write what. Claude or ChatGPT could sit in the deep-work seat tomorrow. What is product-specific is the bot roster and connectors — not the bridge pattern.

---

How trust expands (this is the part most stacks skip):

A bot earns scope by proving the scope it already has. First work should be frequent, reversible, and checkable in about thirty seconds. Wrong output should cost nothing outside. We expand after evidence — not after a cool demo that was 80% right once and never audited.

Where the work spans three apps and a dozen clicks, we stop writing novels. We show the task once (teach / record), then put the reusable skill on a schedule. Description is for judgment. Demonstration is for the click-path. Mixing those up is how you burn a morning "prompting" something you could have clicked through in forty seconds.

---

Interrupt rule, clean:

I don't get pinged unless it's money or the product.

(Or missing data. Or work outside agreed scope.) Everything else: finish, show the result, attach the tape. Park sends, prices, and publishes. I paste LinkedIn myself. External mail stays with me unless I explicitly okay a concrete send.

Held decisions land in one place — a single approval queue CoS checks once a day — not five specialist threads competing for attention. If the queue stays fat, the rules are too tight on reversible work, not that I need to check more often.

Fewer leaks. Not more bots.

---

Material work has a bar before anyone says "done."

Tape: sources, guesses, skipped, evidence.  
Do-alone vs park-for-Simon — so agents don't ask permission to draft, and don't "helpfully" publish. Reversibility is the line: finish what you can undo in under a minute; park what you can't.  
QA gate plus an 8-point self-check before CLEAR — including on substantial internal work, not only outbound letters.

If a source is missing, empty, unreachable, or older than the decision needs: report that and stop. Inventing a confident fill is not clever. It is a leak with better manners.

---

Routines are where automation quietly rots.

Cost hygiene is standing, not a Friday mood. Dedicated cost bot. Weekly audit. Connectors before browser. No idle sessions burning credits while nothing moves.

No new routine until retries and failure cases are defined (rung-6) — a schedule on an unproven process just fails on a timer. When we create a routine we also write the kill condition while calm: the tripwire that pauses it before it becomes a post-mortem. Every run that matters gets a ceiling (volume), an anomaly stop, and a log even when nothing happened — a silent routine is one you cannot audit.

Friday asks a blunt question of every bot: which of your routines should I turn off? Automations that produce output nobody opens never announce it. Killing them is part of the system, not a mood.

Attention vs intention: we filter by what I actually open and answer — mail, calendar, chat, bridge — not by Monday's heroic wishlist. Divergence between written priorities and real attention is a finding. We surface it. We don't paper over it.

Projects don't inherit each other. One project can know the org map; the next one will not — by design. Standing setup lives above projects: shared user memory for the facts, a reusable skill for the how-to, live handoffs still through the Drive bridge. Copy-pasting "how we work" into every project folder is how truth drifts.

---

What we don't do:

• Auto-send external mail  
• Grow a swarm because someone posted a tip  
• Endorsement-theatre around third-party "build a company in 3 days" threads — we mine process, we don't cosplay the broadcast  
• Invent when data is stale  
• Claim we invented Chiefs of Staff, cost bots, teach-by-watching, or shared inboxes

The tips others are still posting — CoS routing, cost hygiene, stale-data honesty, rung-6, attention over intention, earn-scope, show-don't-only-describe — we already run or are folding in. The interesting part is not the tip list. It is whether the system still works when you look away.

AI is powerful. Humans are messy. Today's hype becomes tomorrow's infrastructure you cannot opt out of cleanly. The opposite of "you must join AI now" is not Luddism. It is agency before lock-in: governance that fits in a week, a human in the loop, cash first, near-misses treated as near-misses — not apocalypse theatre.

Truth over theatre. Rotterdam: down to earth, no fluff. Board-worthy when it has to be. Tongue-in-cheek about myself; serious about the problem.

If your agent stack only works when you're watching it like a hawk, you don't have leverage. You have a very expensive pet.

Stop explaining everything. Show the repeatable work once. Earn the next inch of scope. Keep one queue for the irreversible bits. Kill what nobody reads.

We built fewer leaks.
```
