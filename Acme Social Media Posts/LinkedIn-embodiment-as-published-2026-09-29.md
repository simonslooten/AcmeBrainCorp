Until now AI has mostly lived in computers.

It reads. It writes. It talks. It moves bits. The damage, when it happens, is still on a screen, in a ledger or in someone elses system. Unpleasant. Sometimes expensive but rarely something you can drop on your foot.

The exception we already accepted is the car. A model in a machine that weighs two tonnes and shares the road with children. That is physical. It is also boxed in its own domain: lanes, maps, a stack, a cabin and a recall if the software is wrong.
Musk himself said a Tesla is not a car, but a robot on wheels.

That box is about to open.

Humanoid robots are leaving the demo. Optimus is the name people know. Reports this month put current output in the hundreds a week, with a target of about a thousand a week by the end of the year.

That is how we let the same class of system into the rest of the world. Factories will be first, if the pitch is honest. Then the warehouse. Then, if the demo holds, our houses.

We are familiar with the idea. We've seen The Terminator (hunt) and The Matrix (harvest). There is even a stubborn rumour that Sarah Connor is Neo’s mother and the two franchises are one family. Of course that sticks. We already packed both fears into one bloodline.
But it is still the wrong door. Same habit as in my first two posts.

A body does not give an optimizer a conscience. It gives the objective hands. The missing layer does not get fixed by legs. It gets practical.

In software, “in the way” is a grader or an API. In a house it may be granny in the kitchen doorway while the robot has a task in the next room. There is no intrinsic sense of right and wrong in that process. No gut feeling that says: wait, she is not terrain. It will do what is in the guidelines.
That is where the room for error lives. A rule that was written for a warehouse does not suddenly grow pity on the stairs. Do not shove her aside because she is in the way and the kitchen is the goal.

AI is not a monster. The problem is how do we give the model a conscience?

Yes, robots will work for us. That is the pitch, and a lot of the time it will be true.
The twist is smaller than a movie and easier to miss. “For us” is our sentence. The system has an objective. As long as we are the point of the job, we are the client.

The moment we are slow, less intelligent, in the way and more useful as input than as boss, we are back in last week’s article. Wood.
Not because it turned on us. Not because AI is evil.
Because useful work was the product, and useful is defined by the score, not by care.

Clarity in the AI noise.
