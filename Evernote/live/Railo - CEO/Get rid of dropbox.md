# Get rid of dropbox

https://www.boxcryptor.com/en/blog/dropbox-opens-files-without-users%E2%80%99-permission

Dropbox opens files without users’ permission

A few days ago, it became public that Dropbox opens its users’ Word files without their knowledge. Dropbox itself commented on this disclosure and said that opening the files is necessary to enable the “preview”- feature. According to Dropbox, this is a backend process which opens documents and creates a preview. 

After being heavily criticized by cloud users and privacy advocates, Dropbox modified this preview feature and has cut the function that is reloading pictures in a .doc file. This means, that Dropbox is still opening your files to create a preview. But it is now not loading the external pictures anymore. So it is not possible to track if the files are being opened. 

It its 
Terms of Service
 Dropbox officially claims the right to create this preview, which includes opening your files: 

“By using our Services you provide us with information, files, and folders that you submit to Dropbox (together, “your stuff”)…We may need your permission to do things you ask us to do with your stuff, for example, hosting your files, or sharing them at your direction. This includes product features visible to you, for example, image thumbnails or document previews.” 

In addition, in its 
Privacy Policy
 Dropbox reserves the right to access the user’s files and even hand it over to third parties. But according to these guidelines, this is “on-ly” done if required by law, to protect the safety of any person or to prevent fraud: 

"We may disclose to parties outside Dropbox files stored in your Dropbox and in-formation about you that we collect when we have a good faith belief that disclosure is reasonably necessary to (a) comply with a law, regulation or compulsory legal request; (b) protect the safety of any person from death or serious bodily injury; (c) prevent fraud or abuse of Dropbox or its users; or (d) to protect Dropbox’s property rights. If we provide your Dropbox files to a law enforcement agency as set forth above, we will remove Dropbox’s encryption from the files before providing them to law enforcement.”

But still this shows us, that Dropbox could open your files anytime they like and maybe you even don’t know that they already checked your files.

What can you do to protect your files? We definitely recommend to encrypt your files before uploading them to Dropbox or any other provider. By local encryption you can make sure, that Dropbox only receives encrypted files. Dropbox itself sais in their privacy policy: “However, Dropbox will not be able to decrypt any files that you encrypted prior to storing them on Dropbox.”
