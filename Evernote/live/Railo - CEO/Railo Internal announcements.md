Subject: 
Railo management changes and more

All,

As of November of last year, we have made a few changes to the Management Team of The Railo Company.

Let me first state that this has nothing to do with the people that have been with Railo for a long time. We have made these changes to allow them to do what they do best and what they like to do the most, which is: work on and with the technology. The changes made are all around administrative ‘back office’ functions that have much to do with the fact that we see a growing interest from some very interesting (and in some cases very large) organizations.

These organizations demand an administrative back office, contracts, paper trails, legal stuff and more, together with the outstanding technical skills and service that they are already familiar with.

So, we have added a few highly skilled people to the team who, on a part time basis, will deliver those services to Railo.

Gert Franz is the Chief Technology Officer of TRC

Andrew Johnson is responsible for all commercial activities of The Railo Company (TRC)

Robert Rosen is responsible for the legal side of the company

Aurélien Deleusière is heading TRC’s Technical Development Advisory Commission (TDAC)

We are in discussion with a gentlemen to take on responsibility for all Operational tasks at TRC

I have taken on the role of CEO/CFO of The Railo Company

For all others nothing has changed.

Our goal is simple: we want to make Railo the number one CFML platform, with the best technology, the best consultancy and deliver the best support and the best training. And we want not only to maintain the current Status Quo of the CFML market, but we want to grow the market and the Railo platform.

In order to be able to do that, we need to make a few changes / adaptions to the way we work. In this email, I am informing you all on what we are going to change:

1. In or Out?

I have found that a lot of people have a 
getrailo.com
 email address. Most of them do not have any form of contract or agreement in place. As we are building a ‘grown up’ company, out of what was the very best open source CFML engine building team, we need to be aware of our liabilities 
&
 governance. We are going to change the email address policy. Everybody who has a 
getrailo.com
 email address needs to be ‘one of us’. Being ‘one of us’ means that a person needs to have an employment agreement (full-time, part-time or free-lance). All others can not have a 
getrailo.com
 email address. We will take care of presenting everybody with a contract in the next few weeks.

2. 
getrailo.org

getrailo.org
 does not have email addresses. It only points to the Open Source website.

3. Projects, Resources 
&
 Cost allocation

We are going to plan projects and manage them even better than we are today. Internal projects start as a ‘proposal’ which will be discussed in the Board. The Board then approves (or doesn’t) the financial investment and resource alloction that come with that project. After approval, resources are assigned to it and the project is scheduled. Tanja is our project manager. She manages projects, resources, costs and work schedules. TRC has been using Unfuddle for this. We will keep using this tool, until we decide that it doesn’t fit our way of working (anymore). All projects will be managed in Unfuddle.

4. Timesheets

The single most hated thing in companies and yet, one of the most important ones. Especially for companies like TRC, who (at least partly) do ‘time 
&
 material'-based work for customers. As of January 1, 
everybody
 in TRC, employees, free-lancers and external consultants are going to submit weekly time sheets. We will be using a system called Harvest for that. 
railo.harvest.com
 is currently ‘owned’ by Mark. I have already been in contact with Mark about opening this up for the others and to make me an admin. Harvest will eventually be one of the tools that is managed by Project Management. To get this going, I will start managing this for the first period.

5. Communication

We need to structure our public communication. We need to decide on a tone of voice and we need to align ‘stuff’. 

6. New versions of products

As of now, we will not be talking about version numbers 
before
 a product has been released. We will use ‘project names’, just like any other software company. The final version and release date for 
any
 release of 
any
 product will be a Board decision. For every release the Board will consult the tech team, sales, legal and all others involved. Every release will be signed off by the Board.

7. Roadmaps

Roadmaps of products will be created by the tech team, together with Consulting, Sales, Marketing and others. Roadmaps will be presented to the Board 
before
 they are publicly discussed. 
After
 approval by the Board and 
after
 an OK to publicly announce, Product Roadmaps can be discussed.

8. Commercial version of Railo

A very important step in the evolution of Railo will be the addition of a commercial version of the Railo Server. We are currently preparing the launch of this. Stay tuned for more information on this upcoming new offering. New products may be added to this later.

9. Partners

We have added 3 "Strategic Partners” to the eco system. These partners are: Blue River Interactive in the US, Daemon in Australia and Prisma IT in Europe. TRC will focus more on selling products and services through a partner channel in the future. It is likely that more partners will be added.

Guys, I am very much looking forward to work with you all to make this a big success. I know that, at first, stuff like this gets a ‘WTF’ reaction. I know that you feel that you are being monitored and you will get more administrative ’stuff’ on your plates. And you are right. That is exactly what is happening. 

But I also know that a company that has administrative processes in place, operates as a well tuned machine and that all the parts of the machine, the people of TRC, when they know what is expected of them and when they know what ‘the rest’ is doing, can do their job better, will be happier and perform better.

And lastly, as I mentioned before, new customers demand it from us.
