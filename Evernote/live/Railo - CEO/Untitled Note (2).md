# Untitled Note

Malcolm, 

Your email is inaccurate and unsupportable. All authority supports and requires the actions taken by TRC against the GmbH and preclude the positions you seem to be taking on behalf of Blue River to pursue a separate agenda.

We are confident that the facts, events, and circumstances support our stated position and we are equally confident that the appropriate authorities will concur. 

Do I really need to remind you of the fact that you are, under UK law, Legally bound to act in the best interest of a company as a director? So, even if your line of thinking is correct (which it is not) and I am no longer the MD (which I still am, simply because the Board has not come forward with an acceptable replacement, as agreed upon in our agreements), it is your and my obligation as directors to protect TRC’s IP being taken by a minority shareholder. You do not only stand by and watch that happening, you are actively embracing every opportunity to frustrate and block my efforts to do what I must do (by law!).

So, yes, I need to protect the company as a director, because A. it is the law and B. you don’t!

You have made it abundantly clear that you prefer a course of action that benefits only Blue River (and which in turn requires your unwavering support of whatever improper and illegal conduct the GmbH chooses to undertake, despite your having taken the opposite stance only a few months ago). 
Your effort to hide behind your singular and incorrect interpretation of processes, while ignoring the substance of GmbH’s conduct, only further demonstrate where your allegiance lies. 

Y
our position comes to this:  Blue River believes it is in TRC’s best interests for the GmbH to go to a public event like cfcamp, publicly claim that they are the owner of the IP in which BR holds a 10% stake, talk about and/or release Railo 5, change the website to reflect it is Gmbh's, and do whatever else they want to do so long as they continue to provide services (or at least a plausible story) for Blue River and Mura.  

Clearly, the time for these kind of informal emails has come and gone. 

The time has come for you to defend your/Blue River's position rather than just obstruct and delay TRC’s efforts through endless emails. 

If you feel you have a basis for any of the statements you have made, there are appropriate, formal ways to communicate these and agreed upon forums for them to be heard. In fact, despite repeated requests to mediate these claims, you and Blue River have chosen only to delay and to obstruct efforts by TRC to recover amounts owed, protect its intellectual property, serve its existing customer base, and develop new business.  

At a minimum,  you should provide not idle speculation and unfounded accusations but rather an official defense of your position in writing, either from your counsel or, at the very least, on Blue River letterhead, that you are not instructing or communicating with the GmbH and that you have somehow complied with your fiduciary obligations under UK law both as a shareholder and director of TRC, especially given the following facts — only as a starting point: 

1) Blue River is a minority shareholder who has repeatedly sought liquidation of the company as the only course of action;  

2) Blue River has refused to accept any other approach offered for advancing the interests of TRC;  

3) Blue River, holding only a 10% stake, rejected a written offer from the majority shareholder that would have made Blue River whole; 

4) Blue River continues to obstruct and delay all efforts to protect and pursue the economic and legal interests of TRC; and 

5) Blue River supports and stands with (if not instructs) the GmbH’s efforts to violate its obligations and to improperly seize the assets of TRC. 

On this last point 5, we strongly encourage you not to advise in any capacity the GmbH to “ignore" our demands or to otherwise hinder TRC’s efforts to protect its interests. TRC cannot sit idly by and watch as one of its minority shareholders try to run off with the company’s assets while the other minority shareholder blocks any efforts to stop them.  

For far too long, your strategy has been simply to buy time and act as a proxy for the GmbH. Time is up. You have left no other option than to proceed with formal litigation, the steps for which are currently in progress.

The written demands made by TRC and its counsel to the GmbH stand, as do the deadline of next Friday and the consequences for a failure to comply.
